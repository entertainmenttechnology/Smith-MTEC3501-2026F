# MTEC 3501 — Preparing for the Week 2 Face-to-Face Class

*Assigned: Week 1 — Due: before Week 2*

---

## Purpose

During Week 1, you began identifying the project you may want to develop through MTEC 3501 and ENT 4501.

By the beginning of our Week 2 face-to-face class, you should be prepared to describe a **specific current project concept**.

This does **not** mean that your project is final.

Nothing is set in stone.

We are still working within the **Speculate** phase of project development. During the next several classes, you will use brainstorming, discussion, exploratory research, precedent investigation, and other techniques to expand, challenge, combine, revise, or even replace parts of your original idea.

The purpose of this assignment is simply to establish a meaningful starting point.

> **Your systems should be ready. Your project should be ready to change.**

---

## What to Bring to Week 2

Come prepared to explain your current project concept to the class.

Your project may still be broad or ambitious, but someone listening to you should be able to understand what you are currently imagining.

At minimum, be prepared to address the following:

### What is the project?
Describe what you currently imagine creating, designing, researching, performing, building, or developing.

### Why are you interested in it?
What makes this idea worth spending significant time investigating?

### What might someone experience?
If another person encountered the project, what might they see, hear, do, understand, or experience?

### What do you not know yet?
Identify at least one important question, uncertainty, or problem that still needs investigation.

You are not expected to have complete answers. Identifying what you **do not yet know** is an important part of project development.

---

## Project Scale

You will have encountered the concepts of **North Star Vision**, **Minimum Viable Product**, and **Proof of Concept** during Week 1.

You do not need to define these precisely for your project yet.

However, begin thinking about the difference between:

> what the project might ultimately become,

> what a meaningful realizable version might look like,

> and what you may eventually need to demonstrate in order to prove that the project is feasible.

These ideas will continue to evolve throughout the course.

## What Will Happen in Week 2?

Week 2 is our first face-to-face class.

You will begin by sharing and discussing the project concepts you developed during Week 1.

We will then introduce more deliberate **brainstorming methods** and use them to expand the project space.

The objective will not be to select a final answer immediately. Instead, we will ask questions such as:

- What else could this project become?
- What assumptions are hidden inside the current idea?
- What possibilities have not yet been considered?
- What happens if we change the audience, medium, technology, scale, or context?
- What precedents or research might change our understanding of the problem?

Brainstorming remains part of the **Speculate** phase. Research will increasingly interact with speculation, but we are not yet trying to freeze the project into a final proposal.
