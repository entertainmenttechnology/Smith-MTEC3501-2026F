# MTEC 3501 — Week 8 Asynchronous Assignment (Draft)
## Post-Presentation Feedback and PoC Development

**Format:** Independent asynchronous work for October 22, 2026  
**Due:** Before the Week 9 class meeting on October 29, 2026  
**Project horizon:** Seven remaining instructional weeks until the final PoC presentation

## Purpose

The mid-semester presentations are complete. Use this week to respond to presentation feedback, review formative feedback on your GitHub repository, clarify your Proof of Concept (PoC) and technology needs, and **begin practical development**.

The PoC is not due with this assignment. It is the target of the remaining seven instructional weeks. For this course, the PoC is the focused demonstration requiring the least effort needed to show that important system components can be used, manipulated, or deployed. It is not the complete North Star Vision or the eventual Least Viable Product (LVP).

## Guided Asynchronous Activities

These activities are independent and untimed. You do not need to be online or working at a particular moment during the scheduled class period. Revisit them or adjust their order to suit your project.

### 1. Reconsider Presentation and Repository Feedback

Review your presentation evaluation and any formative instructor feedback on your repository. Identify observations that affect your scope, research, technical approach, or documentation. Decide which suggestions to adopt, adapt, investigate, or decline, and briefly explain why.

Clarify what your PoC needs to demonstrate. Distinguish its essential capabilities from features reserved for the longer-term project.

### 2. Examine and Improve Your Repository

Use the repository feedback to identify what is already appropriate, what needs development, and what is not yet present. Correct straightforward problems, update the README and navigation as needed, and track more substantial work in your action plan.

The instructor's 2–1–0 indicators are formative descriptions, not a score or grade:

- **2** — meets current expectations
- **1** — exists but needs improvement
- **0** — absent, even in preliminary form

### 3. Specify the Technology Stack and Its Relationships

Identify the hardware, software, materials, tools, and production processes needed for the PoC. Distinguish components that are available and tested, those proposed but not yet verified, and unresolved requirements. Explain how the components interact; a simple signal-flow, system, or production-workflow diagram may help.

Identify important dependencies and the technical or creative unknowns most likely to impede progress.

### 4. Begin Using a PoC Component

**Do practical work, not only planning.** Select a feasible starting activity using a real component, tool, material, or workflow required by your PoC. For example, install and configure software, acquire and manipulate sample media, establish a signal path, test a sensor, create a simple interaction, or try a basic integration.

The initial attempt need not succeed. It should be relevant to the PoC and teach you something concrete. Record what you attempted, what happened, what you learned, and what you intend to try next. Retain relevant evidence when appropriate, such as code, screenshots, photos, audio, video, or test notes.

### 5. Organize the Path to the Final PoC

Develop or revise an action plan covering the remaining seven instructional weeks. Identify meaningful tasks, dependencies, milestones, resources, critical unknowns, and time for testing, revision, documentation, and final presentation preparation.

Use the GitHub Issues workflow introduced in the course: review existing Issues, update them, and create new Issues for actionable work as appropriate. Issues should help manage the plan, not merely duplicate it.

## Assignment: Evidence of Progress

Before the due date, update your repository to show meaningful progress in these areas:

1. **Feedback and repository:** Record how relevant presentation feedback influenced your decisions; address manageable repository deficiencies and identify remaining corrections.
2. **PoC and technology stack:** State what the PoC will demonstrate and document its components, processes, dependencies, and unresolved technical choices.
3. **Practical development:** Begin hands-on work with at least one essential component or process. Document the attempt and results; a functioning PoC is not required.
4. **Action plan:** Establish or update the seven-week roadmap, including dependencies, and use GitHub Issues to organize actionable next steps.

Use existing repository documents and your development log where possible. Do not create separate submissions merely to repeat information already documented. Keep the relevant work accessible from your repository README.

## Submission

Submit your work in your project repository before the Week 9 class meeting on October 29, 2026. Use existing project documentation and Issues where possible. Make sure the repository and any evidence of your practical trial are discoverable from your README or project board.
