# MTEC 3501 — Agile Sprint 1: Plan, Develop, Assess

## Purpose

In this assignment, you will apply Agile principles to your existing culmination project. Use **SRDMPA (Speculate, Research, Design, Make, Present, Assess)** to guide your development, and use your existing GitHub repository to organize and document the work.

The objective is not simply to plan another stage of your project. **Complete a small, meaningful cycle of development** that produces evidence, advances your project, and helps you determine what should happen next.

**Central question:** How can you organize a short period of development so that it produces demonstrable progress and informs your next decisions?

## 1. Review Your Current Project Status

Examine your project roadmap, GitHub Issues, design documentation, and current prototype or proof of concept. Identify one aspect of the project that can realistically advance during the coming week.

Possible areas include resolving a technical uncertainty, testing a design alternative, producing a functional component, or improving an existing prototype.

## 2. Define a Sprint Goal

Describe an achievable **outcome**, not merely a list of activities. Establish **acceptance criteria**: what evidence would demonstrate that the goal has been achieved?

| Activity-oriented description | Outcome-oriented sprint goal |
| --- | --- |
| Work on my sensor system. | Demonstrate that sensor data can reliably control one sound parameter. |
| Research projection techniques. | Select and validate a projection approach against defined requirements. |
| Improve my interface. | Produce and test a revised interface addressing two identified usability problems. |

The goal should be achievable within the week even though your larger project remains incomplete.

## 3. Organize the Work in GitHub

Use your existing project repository. Identify or create GitHub Issues for the work needed to reach your sprint goal. Where useful, organize them in a GitHub Project board with **Backlog**, **In Progress**, and **Done** statuses.

There is no required number of issues. Choose a level of detail appropriate to your project and the work you intend to undertake.

## 4. Execute the Development Cycle

Carry out substantive project work using SRDMPA. The stages are not a rigid sequence: research may lead to renewed speculation; making may expose a design problem; assessment may prompt another cycle.

Document what actually happens, including unexpected results. Do not reconstruct an idealized sequence after the fact.

A sprint may produce a working prototype, experiment, design revision, test result, or well-supported decision. Not every meaningful outcome is a finished artifact.

## 5. Present and Assess the Outcome

At the end of the week, make your outcome visible in your repository and respond to these questions:

1. **Intent and result:** What did you intend to accomplish, and what actually happened?
2. **Learning:** What did you learn that changes or confirms your understanding of the project?
3. **Next decision:** What should happen next? Does the evidence suggest a change in design, process, scope, or roadmap?

Also identify whether your work produced an **iteration**, an **increment**, or **both**:

- **Iteration:** You revisited and refined an approach or understanding.
- **Increment:** You added a demonstrable, usable, or testable project capability.

Explain your classification briefly and link it to the evidence. A sprint that misses its original goal can still be productive when it yields useful findings and a justified next step.

## Submission

Submit a **link to a GitHub page or issue** that makes the following easy to find:

- Your sprint goal and acceptance criteria, recorded before development.
- Your development record, with links to relevant issues, commits, experiments, artifacts, test results, or other evidence.
- Your outcome and assessment, including what you learned, what you plan to do next, and whether the sprint produced an iteration, an increment, or both.

Keep the written record concise when the supporting evidence is already clear and accessible. Your GitHub repository remains the authoritative project record; no separate report is required.

## Formative Review Criteria

| Criterion | What will be examined |
| --- | --- |
| **Goal definition** | Is the sprint appropriately scoped, with meaningful acceptance criteria? |
| **Development** | Is there evidence of substantive project work? |
| **Assessment** | Does the student distinguish observations, results, and conclusions? |
| **Adaptation** | Is the proposed next step justified by what was learned? |

**Successful completion of the original sprint goal is not, by itself, the primary measure of success.** Well-documented experimentation and evidence-based decisions are valuable outcomes.

## Looking Ahead

The sprint covers the interval between class meetings, not just the scheduled class period. At the next meeting, be prepared to briefly demonstrate your outcome and discuss what changed in your understanding of the project.

This is an introduction to a reusable Agile-informed practice, not a requirement that every subsequent week follow formal Scrum procedures. Use the techniques where they help you develop, test, and assess your project.
