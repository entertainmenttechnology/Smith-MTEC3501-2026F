# MTEC 3501 — Asynchronous Learning Activities and PoC Development Assignment

**Format:** Independent asynchronous work; materials released at the beginning of the scheduled class period  
**Next meeting:** Progress discussion at the following synchronous class  
**Project horizon:** Approximately seven weeks until the final PoC presentation

## Purpose

You have presented your Culmination Project proposal, received feedback, and had time to respond. This week you will continue that process, review formative feedback on your GitHub repository, establish or refine your technology stack, and **begin practical development of your Proof of Concept (PoC)**.

The PoC is **not due at our next meeting**. It is the target of the remaining seven weeks. For our purposes, the PoC seeks the least amount of effort needed to demonstrate the ability to use, manipulate, or deploy the required system components. It is not the complete North Star Vision or the eventual MVP.

## Part I — Guided asynchronous learning activities

The following activities guide your independent work. They are **not timed exercises** and do not require you to be online or working at a particular moment during the scheduled class period. You may revisit them or adjust their order to suit your project.

### 1. Reconsider feedback and your PoC

Revisit your presentation evaluation and consider the changes you have already made since presenting. Review the instructor's formative repository feedback as well. Identify observations that affect your scope, research, technical approach, or documentation. Decide which suggestions to adopt, adapt, investigate, or decline, and why.

Clarify what your PoC needs to demonstrate and distinguish its essential capabilities from features that belong to the longer-term project.

### 2. Examine and improve your repository

Use the repository feedback to identify components that are already appropriate, those that need development, and those not yet present. Correct straightforward problems, update the README and navigation as needed, and identify more substantial work for your action plan. The repository should document your project's development and make relevant files and external resources accessible.

The instructor's 2–1–0 indicators are **formative descriptions**, not a score or grade: 2 = meets current expectations; 1 = exists but needs improvement; 0 = absent, even in preliminary form.

### 3. Specify the technology stack and its relationships

Identify the actual hardware, software, materials, tools, and production processes needed for the PoC. Distinguish components that are available and tested, those proposed but not yet verified, and unresolved requirements. Consider how the components interact; a simple signal-flow, system, or production-workflow diagram may help.

Identify important dependencies and the technical or creative unknowns most likely to impede progress.

### 4. Begin using the components of your PoC

**Do practical work, not only planning.** Select a feasible starting activity using a real component, tool, material, or workflow required by your PoC. Examples include installing and configuring software, acquiring and manipulating sample media, establishing a signal path, testing a sensor, creating a simple interaction, or trying a basic integration.

The initial attempt need not succeed. What matters is that it is relevant to the PoC and that you learn something concrete from it. Record what you attempted, what happened, what you learned, and what you intend to try next. Retain evidence when appropriate, such as code, screenshots, photos, audio, video, or test notes.

### 5. Organize the path to the final PoC

Develop or revise an action plan covering the remaining seven weeks. Identify meaningful tasks, dependencies, milestones, resources, critical unknowns, and time for testing, revision, documentation, and final presentation preparation.

Use the GitHub Issues workflow previously introduced in the course: review existing Issues, update them, and create new Issues for actionable work as appropriate. Issues should help manage the plan, not merely duplicate its text.

## Part II — Assignment: Work to advance before our next meeting

Continue the work initiated through the guided activities. By the following synchronous class, your repository should reflect meaningful progress in these areas:

1. **Feedback and repository:** Integrate relevant presentation feedback, address manageable repository deficiencies, and identify remaining corrections.
2. **PoC and technology stack:** State what the PoC will demonstrate and document the components, processes, and unresolved technical choices involved.
3. **Practical development:** Begin hands-on work with at least one essential component or process. Document your attempt and its results; a functioning PoC is **not** required.
4. **Action plan:** Establish or update the seven-week roadmap, including dependencies, and use GitHub Issues to organize actionable next steps.

Use existing repository documents and your development log where possible; do not create separate submissions merely to repeat the same information. Continue independent project work as appropriate to your particular PoC.

## Part III — At the following synchronous class

Be prepared to report briefly, using your repository and any available development evidence:

- **What changed?** Which feedback influenced your decisions or documentation?
- **What did you do?** What actual work did you begin on your PoC?
- **What did you discover?** What worked, failed, or remains uncertain about the technology stack or workflow?
- **What comes next?** Which tasks, dependencies, and GitHub Issues will guide your immediate work?

This is a progress report and discussion, **not the PoC deadline**.
